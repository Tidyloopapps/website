How to ship samples and swatches separately in Shopify (letter mail)

Updated November 26, 2026

Fabric swatches, paint chips, flooring samples, perfume vials and similar small items fit in a flat envelope that costs far less to mail than a parcel, and they often ship from a different shelf or the same day the order comes in. When a customer orders swatches together with a full product, packing everything in one box means the samples wait for the product and ride at parcel rates. The goal is two shipments inside one order: the samples in an envelope, the rest as a normal parcel.

What Shopify does on its own

  • Shopify puts every item from the same location into one fulfillment group, so samples and full-size products show up as one shipment to pack.
  • You can split them by hand: on the order page, use Fulfill items for the samples first and mark them shipped by letter mail, then fulfill the rest as a parcel.
  • Shopify Flow can tag orders that contain sample products, but it has no action that moves those items into their own fulfillment group.

Splitting by hand works for a few orders a week. When samples are a regular part of your sales, every packer has to spot them on every order, and a missed one either delays the samples or puts them in a box at parcel cost.

Doing it automatically

  1. Tag every sample or swatch product with a shared product tag, for example Sample.
  2. Add a rule: "When an item has the tag Sample, ship it separately."

Tidyloop Split applies that rule to each new order inside the same order. Full-size items stay in the normal shipment; each sample gets its own fulfillment group, so packers see it as a separate envelope shipment. "Ship separately" works on every plan.

Tidyloop Split does not buy postage or pick a carrier. It only separates the items so each shipment can go by the right service.

Good practice

  • Tag the product, not the order, so every future order is covered.
  • Give sample products an accurate weight, so Shopify can show a letter-mail rate when the cart has only samples.
  • Check your postal service's size and thickness limits for flat envelopes; a sample that is too thick is charged as a parcel.
  • Say on the product page that samples ship separately and may arrive before the rest of the order.
